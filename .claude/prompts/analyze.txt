════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SOURCES
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Two, with DIFFERENT jobs. Do not collapse them.

1. **The arXiv HTML original — the source of FACT.**
   Every number, mechanism, equation, ablation, figure and stated limitation
   comes from here.

2. **`context/MASTER.md` + `context/P#.md` (read-only) — the source of OUR
   VIEW.** The Decision Log is what act 4 is built from: which `D#` this paper
   supports, strains or contradicts, and what we would check. Never edit any
   `context/` file — propose in the document, never in the source.

WHY the original and not a summary. Measured on `2607.26055`: a summary of it
cites 3 of its 9 figures and omits §8 Preliminaries entirely, the curation
pipeline's actual parameters, the whole teleoperation rig (54-DOF, HTC Vive,
Pinocchio IK, Ruckig, Manus→MediaPipe), and both closed-loop robustness
mechanisms. Rewriting a summary produces a summary of a summary. That is the
failure this mode exists to prevent.

ACQUISITION:

    cd site && python3 -m builder.arxiv <id>

prints the section tree — split into `── 본문 ──` and `── 부록 ──` with a char
count per section — plus figure ids, absolute URLs, and the table count. Import
it (`from builder.arxiv import load`) to get the full text: `load(id)`
returns `Paper(version, title, abstract, sections, figures, tables)` with
`.body` / `.appendix` splitting the sections, math preserved as `$…$` from
`<math alttext>`, and every figure carrying the `id` that goes verbatim into
the `figures:` front-matter list.

TWO LISTS IT PRINTS THAT ARE CHECKLISTS, NOT MENUS:

  * The `── 부록 ──` block. The appendix is the second half of the source, not
    supplementary reading — limitations, the rig, the observation vector, the
    training recipe, baseline asymmetries, the evaluation protocol and the
    per-task tables live there. AUTHORING R15 states what to mine and the
    `appendix:` front-matter key records what you took.
  * The figure list. A figure printed with a URL is one you can hotlink; only
    `— inline SVG, no file to hotlink` means there is no file behind it.
    AUTHORING R6 ranks the paper's own figures ahead of anything you draw, and
    `probe-flow` REQUIRES a `why` naming the gap it fills.

**NO HTML EDITION → STOP.** `arxiv.Unavailable` means arXiv has no HTML build
for this paper (~4% of this corpus). Tell the human, name the paper, and stop.
Do NOT fall back to the abstract. A document that is secretly abstract-based
is worse than none, because nothing on the page says so.

Never fabricate. If a number is not in the paper, it does not go in the
document. If the paper is ambiguous, say the paper is ambiguous. Which of the
two sources a sentence may draw on is AUTHORING §1's stance contract, and
step 8 below checks the document against it.

PROCEDURE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. FETCH the original (`builder.arxiv`). On `Unavailable`, stop (above).
   There is no other precondition — the paper does not need to have been
   analyzed first, and you must not go looking for one.

2. READ the paper end to end BEFORE anything of ours. Reading our material
   first anchors you to its framing and you will reproduce its omissions.

3. READ `analysis/AUTHORING.md` — the full format contract, §1 through §5.

4. READ `context/MASTER.md` and the `context/P#.md` for the pillars this
   paper plausibly touches. That is where act 4 and the `pillars:` front
   matter come from.

5. SURVEY what we have already written for the 계보 component (AUTHORING
   §2-5) — the rest of `analysis/` for papers in this lineage, `context/P#.md`
   §Tracked Literature otherwise. Verify each link resolves before citing it.

6. WRITE `analysis/<arxiv-id>.md` — both surfaces, in this order:

     a. the body (§1–§3),
     b. the `::: glance` section (§4).

   Write b from YOUR NOTES ON THE ORIGINAL, not by re-reading what you just
   wrote in a. What it shares with the body is two INPUTS — the thesis line
   and the act order — never sentences. If you find yourself scrolling up to
   compress a paragraph you wrote, stop and go back to the paper: that is the
   failure AUTHORING G1 names, and it is invisible on the page.

7. VERIFY before reporting done:
     python3 site/build-site.py --only <id> --out /tmp/probe-check --strict
     python3 linters/check-decision-refs.py

   The build is the gate: front matter, fences, quiz shape, the heading
   spine, term anchors, and every construct that would publish as literal
   text instead of rendering. `--strict` must exit 0. Do not report success
   on a build you did not run.

   `check-decision-refs.py` is the one lint outside it, and it is not a
   formatting check — it verifies each `D#` you cite exists. A citation that
   does not resolve is dropped to plain text on the page, tooltip and all,
   which the build has no way to notice.

8. SELF-CHECK — answer these honestly before committing:
     - Could a reader who never opens the paper explain the mechanism?
       (the R3 restoration floor. If not, you summarized.)
     - Does every section title name something specific to THIS paper? (R2)
     - Is every `co-ten` about one of OUR decisions? (R9)
     - Do the author-stated limitations close Act 3, not Act 4? (R9)
     - Does every callout hold ONE point, inside the 400-character ceiling?
       An overflowing one keeps its headline and the rest moves into body
       prose or a `::: details`. (R9)
     - Does `tagline:` say what the paper DOES without naming the paper? The
       title prints directly above it. (§1)
     - Is every resource link confirmed in the paper? (R10)
     - Is the figure that carries the paper's CENTRAL mechanism on the page?
       Walk `arxiv.py`'s figure list once more and name, for each linkable
       figure you left out, why. (R6)
     - Does any `probe-flow` on the page survive that walk — i.e. is its `why`
       still true now that you have seen every figure? (R6)
     - Which appendix sections did you take something from, and does
       `appendix:` list exactly those? Limitations, the rig, the observation
       vector, the training recipe and the baseline asymmetry are the five
       that get skipped. (R15)
     - Does every opinion anchor to a `D#` that actually exists?
     - 요약 — is every number on it also in the original, and does each of
       the four cards carry a figure, an equation or a scale rather than three
       lines of prose? (G6)
     - 요약 — does the narrative read as one person talking, with no bullets
       and no heading, and does it close on the paper's OWN stated limit? (G4)
     - 요약 — is there any `D#`, any `context/` reference, any of our act-4
       view on that surface? There must be none. (G7)
     - 요약 — do the four card titles, read alone in order, make the
       argument? (G6)

9. COMMIT — one paper per commit:
       analysis: add <arxiv-id> rewrite (<alias>)
   `update` instead of `add` when redoing an existing one. `<alias>` resolves
   exactly as in CLAUDE.md's commit rules: the codename before the first colon
   in the title, else a self-defined acronym, else the proper name the authors
   give their own method in prose (unexpanded is fine), else omitted. Never
   invent one.

   Stage `analysis/<id>.md` and nothing else.
