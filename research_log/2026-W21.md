## 🥇 Paper 1 — PRIORITY ★★★

**Learning Versatile Humanoid Manipulation with Touch Dreaming**
[arXiv:2604.13015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13015) · Yaru Niu, Zhenlong Fang, Binghong Chen, Shuai Zhou, Revanth Senthilkumaran, Hao Zhang, Bingqing Chen, Chen Qiu et al. · submitted: April 14, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep (8-week window)

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D4 (tactile fusion strategy), D6 (proprioception split).

D4 is the primary target. D4.v1 is late fusion (hand head only) requiring tactile sensors at inference. The "touch dreaming" auxiliary objective is a structurally different training paradigm: no tactile sensors are needed at inference, yet the Transformer trunk learns contact-aware representations because it is trained to predict future hand-joint forces AND future tactile latents alongside action chunks — all from within the behavioral cloning training loop. This differs from HapticVLA's distillation (W20): HapticVLA uses a teacher-student pipeline; touch dreaming imposes the auxiliary prediction target directly on the primary policy trunk during BC. The decisive empirical distinction is the 30% relative gain of **latent-space** prediction over raw tactile prediction, which shows the trunk learns compact contact geometry rather than noisy high-dimensional sensor readouts.

D6: hand-joint force prediction as an auxiliary proprioception objective provides implicit split-proprioception supervision without architectural surgery. The trunk learns to reason about finger-level force state alongside arm state from a unified observation, representing a lightweight alternative to D6 option (iii) "hand-prop direct injection."

CP2 relevance: if in-hand rotation shows tactile sensors to be unreliable at real deployment, touch dreaming provides a training-time-only supervision path — sensors present at CP1 training, absent at CP2 evaluation — consistent with D4.v1 late fusion.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

Predicting future tactile signals in **latent space** (not raw space) as a behavioral cloning auxiliary objective — unlike TacFiLM and HapticVLA (both in tracked literature), which require inference-time tactile sensors or distill a compact token through a teacher, touch dreaming imposes no inference-time sensor constraint and is validated to outperform both raw-signal prediction (+30% relative) and vision-only BC (+90.9% relative) on real humanoid contact-rich tasks across five task categories.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

If touch dreaming transfers from humanoid BC to the P1 dexterous hand head: in `hand_head_cfg.py`, add `auxiliary_loss = "touch_dreaming"` and set `touch_dreaming_targets = ["hand_joint_forces_future_1step", "tactile_latent_future_1step"]`. The hand head trains with three simultaneous targets: action chunk, future hand-joint force (1 step), future tactile latent (1 step). Metric to validate at CP1 sim: contact-precision on in-hand rotation (fingertip position error at grasp transition) with vs. without auxiliary targets. Adoption threshold: ≥ 15% contact-precision improvement over action-chunk-only BC at CP1 sim promotes touch dreaming to default D4.v1 variant.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

Touch dreaming is validated on a humanoid whole-body Transformer trunk attending to multi-view RGB + proprioception + action chunks — a high-capacity, richly conditioned network. P1's hand head at CP1 is an MLP (D8.v1), which may lack the capacity to jointly learn action generation and latent-space tactile prediction. Cheapest sanity check: replace the MLP hand head with a 3-layer Transformer at CP1 sim entry and add the touch dreaming auxiliary loss; compare vs. MLP hand head without auxiliary loss on in-hand rotation. If MLP-based hand head fails to improve with touch dreaming, the D8 hand-head architecture decision (MLP vs. Transformer) becomes gated on the auxiliary-loss result, which must be resolved before CP1 code commit.

## 🥈 Paper 2 — PRIORITY ★★

**OmniVTA: Visuo-Tactile World Modeling for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation**
[arXiv:2603.19201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19201) · Yuhang Zheng, Songen Gu, Weize Li, Yupeng Zheng, Yujie Zang, Shuai Tian, Xiang Li, Ce Hao et al. · submitted: March 23, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep (8-week window)

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D4 (tactile fusion strategy), D8 (sharing module mechanism).

D4 is the primary target. D4.v1 is static late fusion at a single inference step. OmniVTA restructures tactile integration as a **predictive contact-evolution loop**: a two-stream visuo-tactile world model predicts short-horizon contact state evolution (visual stream + tactile stream in parallel), a contact-aware fusion policy generates actions conditioned on predicted contact evolution, and a 60Hz reflexive controller closes the loop by correcting deviations between predicted and observed tactile signals. This predictive-corrective architecture is structurally distinct from all four D4 options currently in the decision log (early fusion, late fusion, distillation, touch dreaming).

D8: OmniVTA's two-stream architecture (visual stream + tactile stream feeding a fusion policy) is a concrete reference for D8 option "(iii) both" — body hidden state + action jointly informing the sharing module. The parallel-stream fusion with a corrective controller demonstrates that multi-stream architectures scale to 86 tasks and generalize to unseen objects without routing instability.

CP1 relevance: OmniVTA's contact-aware fusion policy is architecturally compatible with a VLA decoder that has separate body and hand streams. The contact-evolution prediction could augment the FiLM D8.v1 sharing module at CP1, conditioning the hand head on predicted contact state alongside body action `a_b`.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

A forward-prediction world model that explicitly models short-horizon **contact-state evolution** (not just object trajectory or action sequence) paired with a 60Hz reflexive corrective controller — unlike TacFiLM and HapticVLA (both passive tactile fusion), OmniVTA closes the tactile loop at control frequency, validated on 21,000+ trajectories across 86 tasks with generalization to unseen objects and geometric configurations.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

If OmniVTA's predictive-corrective loop applies to P1: in `hand_head_cfg.py`, add `tactile_loop_mode = "predictive_corrective"` as a new D4 deferred option (option F). Concrete action item before CP1: at FiLM sharing module entry, evaluate whether `a_b` alone (D8.v1) or `a_b + predicted_contact_latent` (OmniVTA-inspired augmentation) is a better conditioning signal for the hand head. Metric: per-finger contact precision and in-hand rotation success at CP1 sim, compared to D4.v1 baseline. The OmniViTac dataset (21k trajectories, 86 tasks, code/data/models promised for public release) is the largest open contact-rich tactile dataset to date; download before CP1 for tactile encoder pre-training if D4 early fusion is reconsidered at CP5.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

OmniVTA's reflexive controller runs at 60Hz, requiring the world model forward pass to complete in < 16ms. This is feasible for a gripper but may not match the latency budget for a 20-DoF dexterous hand coordinating distributed fingertip sensors simultaneously. At CP1 sim entry, measure world model forward pass latency in the Isaac Lab environment against `robot_cfg.hand_control_freq_hz`. If latency > 16ms on the target GPU, the predictive-corrective loop degrades to open-loop and its primary benefit vanishes — at which point D4.v1 late fusion remains the correct default.

## 🥉 Paper 3 — PRIORITY ★

**DexViTac: Collecting Human Visuo-Tactile-Kinematic Demonstrations for Contact-Rich Dexterous Manipulation**
[arXiv:2603.17851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17851) · Xitong Chen, Yifeng Pan, Min Li, Xiaotian Ding · submitted: March 18, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep (8-week window)

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D4 (tactile fusion strategy), D6 (proprioception split).

D4: DexViTac introduces a **kinematics-grounded tactile representation** that resolves semantic ambiguity in tactile signals by conditioning the tactile encoder on current hand joint positions. This is a minimal, non-architectural change to D4.v1 (late fusion): the tactile encoder receives joint positions as context before producing the tactile feature that feeds the hand head. The system collects synchronized visuotactile + kinematic demonstrations at 248+ episodes/hour, and policies trained on this joint dataset achieve 85%+ success on 4 contact-rich dexterous tasks, with a 25% improvement over the vision-only baseline attributable specifically to kinematic-conditioned tactile observations.

D6: kinematic grounding directly ties proprioception (joint positions) to tactile signal interpretation — the hand's joint-space state disambiguates which finger surface is contacting and at what force angle. This is a concrete argument for D6 option (iii) "hand-prop direct injection" when tactile sensors are present: finger proprioception should inform the tactile encoder before the hand head rather than remaining a separate encoder branch.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

Grounding the tactile encoder on **current hand joint positions** during both data collection and representation learning — unlike MILE and prior visuotactile systems that treat tactile signals as independent modality tokens, DexViTac shows that the same contact pattern is semantically ambiguous without its corresponding joint configuration, and that kinematic conditioning resolves this ambiguity, yielding 85%+ success on 4 contact-rich tasks vs. baseline without kinematic grounding.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

If kinematic grounding of the tactile encoder is adopted: in `tactile_encoder_cfg.py`, add `kinematic_conditioning = True` with a linear projection of 20-dim hand joint positions concatenated to the tactile feature map before the encoder forward pass. This is a one-line architecture change compatible with D4.v1 (late fusion) that requires no restructuring of the hand head. Metric at CP1 sim: contact precision on in-hand rotation with vs. without kinematic conditioning on the tactile encoder. Adoption threshold: ≥ 10% contact-precision improvement promotes kinematic conditioning to the D4.v1 default encoder configuration.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

DexViTac validates on high-density fingertip visuotactile sensors with rich spatial resolution. The kinematic-grounding benefit relies on sufficient tactile spatial resolution to make joint configuration informationally relevant — for low-resolution tactile (< 10 taxels per finger), kinematics cannot disambiguate sub-finger contact location better than a force-magnitude threshold. Cheapest check at CP1: measure mutual information between tactile signal and hand joint-space configuration using sim TacSL before committing to kinematic conditioning in the encoder. If MI is low (< 0.05 nats per taxel per DoF), the kinematic conditioning adds noise rather than signal.
