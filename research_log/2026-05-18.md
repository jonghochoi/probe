## 🥇 Paper 1 — PRIORITY ★★★

**Demystifying Action Space Design for Robotic Manipulation Policies**
[arXiv:2602.23408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23408) · Yuchun Feng, Jinliang Zheng, Zhihao Wang, Dongxiu Liu, Jianxiong Li, Jiangmiao Pang, Tai Wang, Xianyuan Zhan · submitted: Feb 26, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D2 (wrist boundary), D3 (action expert partition).

D2 is the primary target. D2.v1 is B-1 (wrist→hand, body=joint-space minus wrist). The study systematically compares joint-space vs. task-space (Cartesian EE target) parameterization for the body arm — exactly the B-1 vs. B-2 dimension of the D2 wrist-boundary decision. Key empirical finding: joint-space favors "control stability" (consistent motion, predictable joint trajectories, critical for in-hand rotation where arm tremor destabilizes fingertip contact) while task-space favors "generalization" (cross-environment, cross-arm transfer — the D2 B-2 trigger condition is "hardware transition or cross-arm generalization need," matching CP4+CP5). This is large-scale empirical grounding confirming D2.v1 (B-1, joint-space body) as the correct anchor for CP1 and CP2, with the B-2 migration trigger now backed by data rather than heuristic alone.

D3: The study separately examines absolute vs. delta action representation. "Properly designing the policy to predict delta actions consistently improves performance" across all tested scenarios — this applies to both body and hand expert outputs in D3.v1's slice partition. If delta actions improve learnability, the body expert outputs `Δq_b` (delta joint-space) and the hand expert outputs `Δq_h` (delta hand joint-space), rather than absolute joint targets.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

A 13,000+-rollout real-world bimanual robot empirical study — the first systematic large-scale comparison of absolute vs. delta representation AND joint-space vs. task-space parameterization under identical policy architecture, training budget, and evaluation protocol across 4 task categories and 500+ trained models, demonstrating that delta actions always improve learnability while joint-space/task-space trade-offs (stability vs. generalization) are only resolvable empirically at scale, not inferrable from architectural priors.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

If delta actions consistently improve learnability: in `body_head_cfg.py`, set `action_representation = "delta_joint"` for the body expert output (not absolute `q_b`), and in `hand_head_cfg.py`, set `action_representation = "delta_joint"` for the hand expert output. The FiLM sharing module (D8.v1) receives `Δq_b` as its conditioning signal rather than absolute `q_b` — since `Δq_b` has lower dynamic range than `q_b`, add `film_input_scale = "learned_linear"` in `sharing_module_cfg.py` to recover conditioning amplitude before CP1 code entry. Metric to validate at CP1 sim: compare `abs_joint` vs. `delta_joint` body and hand outputs on in-hand rotation, measuring both success rate and gradient cosine similarity between body/hand losses (D9 monitoring) to confirm delta representation does not amplify gradient conflict.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

The study uses bimanual robot arms — the joint-space vs. task-space comparison is conducted for arm control only, not for a dexterous hand. P1's hand expert (D2.v1 B-1) operates in a 20-DoF finger joint-space; the study provides no evidence on whether delta joint-space is stable for dexterous finger joints under contact loads, where small `Δq` values may be below actuator resolution thresholds. Cheapest check at CP1: run delta vs. absolute hand joint representation on the sim in-hand rotation task; if `Δq_h` < actuator dead-zone on any finger DoF for >20% of steps, add a minimum delta clamp in `hand_action_cfg.py` before committing to delta representation for the hand expert.

## 🥈 Paper 2 — PRIORITY ★★

**WarmPrior: Straightening Flow-Matching Policies with Temporal Priors**
[arXiv:2605.13959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13959) · Sinjae Kang, Chanyoung Kim, Kaixin Wang, Li Zhao, Kimin Lee · submitted: May 13, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D7 (flow matching coordination), D3 (action expert partition).

D7 is the primary target. D7.v1 is hierarchical flow: body expert completes K-step denoising → `a_b` → hand expert denoises conditioned on `a_b`. Each denoising pass currently starts from a standard Gaussian source `N(0, I)`. WarmPrior replaces this with a temporally grounded prior constructed from recent action history (`a_{t-1}` chunk), producing markedly straighter probability paths — the same geometric effect as OT coupling in Rectified Flow but computed at inference time from the robot's own action history rather than from dataset-level coupling, requiring no retraining. Applied to D7.v1: the body expert's source changes from `N(0, I)` to `WarmPrior(a_b_{t-1})`; the hand expert's source becomes `WarmPrior(a_h_{t-1})` optionally blended with `linear_proj(a_b)` as dual temporal grounding. Straighter denoising paths for the body head mean the conditioning signal `a_b` is produced with fewer NFE, reducing latency at the D7.v1 hand-conditioning step.

D3: WarmPrior reshapes the exploration distribution in "prior-space reinforcement learning," improving sample efficiency and final performance. If the body expert is RL-fine-tuned at CP2 following the π_RL pattern (D3 deferred option (iii)), WarmPrior shifts exploration to start near the demonstration manifold, directly reducing wasted gradient steps on physically implausible body actions.

CP1 relevance: the warm prior modification is a one-parameter change to the denoising sampler with no gradient impact; it can be ablated at CP1 sim with zero training cost.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

Replacing the standard Gaussian source with a temporally grounded prior built from recent action history — unlike Rectified Flow (OT coupling at dataset level requiring offline pre-computation), WarmPrior computes its coupling at inference time from the robot's own preceding action chunk, requires no additional data, training changes, or increased NFE, and is validated to improve both behavior cloning success rates AND prior-space RL sample efficiency, identifying the source distribution as a previously overlooked design axis in generative robot control.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

If WarmPrior generalizes to hierarchical flow: in `flow_cfg.py`, set `body_source_distribution = "warm_prior"` and `hand_source_distribution = "warm_prior_conditioned"`, where `warm_prior_conditioned = WarmPrior(a_h_{t-1}) + alpha * linear_proj(a_b)` with `alpha` initialized to 0.5 and tuned at CP1. This is a 2-line change in the denoising sampler, compatible with D7.v1's hierarchical schedule, and requires no retraining. Concrete metric at CP1 sim: NFE to 90% of maximum in-hand rotation success rate, comparing `gaussian_source` vs. `warm_prior` for body head only, then for both body+hand heads. If NFE reduction exceeds 20% for the body head without degrading hand success, set `body_source_distribution = "warm_prior"` as default in `flow_cfg.py` before CP1 rollout.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

WarmPrior assumes recent action history `a_{t-1}` is a reliable proximity anchor for the current denoising trajectory. In D7.v1's hierarchical setting, the hand expert's warm prior may fail at task-phase transitions — grasp approach → contact → in-hand rotation — where `a_h_{t-1}` from the pre-contact phase lies far from the in-contact action manifold. In these cases WarmPrior may bias denoising away from the correct contact regime rather than toward it, producing worse coverage than Gaussian noise at phase boundaries. Cheapest check at CP1 sim: plot denoising trajectory curvature of the hand expert at the pre-contact → contact transition with vs. without WarmPrior; if path curvature increases at transition (i.e., warm prior hurts), limit WarmPrior to the body head only and keep Gaussian source for the hand head.
