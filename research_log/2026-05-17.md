## 🥇 Paper 1 — PRIORITY ★★★

**MolmoAct2: Action Reasoning Models for Real-world Deployment**
[arXiv:2605.02881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02881) · Haoquan Fang, Jiafei Duan, Donovan Clay, Sam Wang, Shuo Liu, Weikai Huang, Xiang Fan, Wei-Chuan Tsai, et al. (AllenAI / University of Washington) · submitted: May 4, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D8 (sharing module mechanism), D7 (flow matching coordination), D5 (backbone freeze strategy).

D8 is the primary target. MolmoAct2's per-layer KV-cache conditioning of the flow-matching action expert on the VLM backbone is exactly D8's deferred option (C) cross-attention at (β) multi-layer depth. D8.v1 is single-point FiLM with `a_b` → (γ, β) at (α) single point; MolmoAct2 routes every VLM layer's key-value pairs into the action expert's corresponding layers, demonstrating consistent improvement across 7 simulation and real-world manipulation benchmarks versus simpler conditioning baselines including π0.5.

D7: MolmoAct2 preserves an independent flow-matching action expert (not coupled denoising), maintaining architectural compatibility with D7.v1 (hierarchical flow: body completes K-step denoising → `a_b` → hand denoises conditioned on `a_b`). The per-layer KV-cache conditioning is a purely information-sharing modification, not a denoising schedule change.

D5: The MolmoER backbone is fine-tuned for spatial and embodied reasoning, then frozen during action-expert training — partially supporting D5.v1 (full freeze) but introducing a pre-freeze specialization phase absent from the standard π0/π0.5 protocol.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

Per-layer KV-cache conditioning of a flow-matching continuous-action expert on a 2.3B discrete-token VLM — unlike π0/π0.5 where the action expert attends to a flat pool of VLM tokens at the output layer, MolmoAct2 routes each VLM layer's KV caches to the corresponding action-expert layer, is validated at scale (5 embodiments, 3 open datasets totaling 720+ hours of bimanual data), and is fully open (weights, training code, all data), while outperforming π0.5 on manipulation tasks and GPT-5 on 13 embodied-reasoning benchmarks.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

If per-layer KV-cache conditioning (D8 option (C)+(β)) generalizes from VLM→action-expert to body-expert→hand-expert conditioning: in `hand_head_cfg.py`, replace `sharing_module = "film_single"` with `sharing_module = "cross_attn_per_layer"`. This requires redesigning the hand head from MLP to a small Transformer — the exact architectural trigger listed in D8's deferred (C) candidate. Concrete action item: lock the MLP vs. Transformer hand-head decision before CP1 code entry, because `cross_attn_per_layer` is incompatible with an MLP-style hand head and cannot be retrofitted without re-implementation.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

MolmoAct2 conditions a flow-matching action expert on language-visual KV caches from a 2.3B VLM — rich, high-dimensional multi-modal token representations. D8 conditions the hand expert on body-expert action output `a_b`, a 7-DoF joint-space vector. The conditioning signal dimensionality and modality differ by orders of magnitude. Cheapest sanity check at CP1: ablate single-point FiLM(`a_b`) vs. 3-layer cross-attention(`a_b`) on the hand head in sim in-hand rotation, measuring cosine similarity of resulting hand trajectories across ablations, before committing to a full Transformer hand-head redesign.

## 🥈 Paper 2 — PRIORITY ★★

**HapticVLA: Contact-Rich Manipulation via Vision-Language-Action Model without Inference-Time Tactile Sensing**
[arXiv:2603.15257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15257) · Konstantin Gubernatorov, Mikhail Sannikov, Ilya Mikhalchuk, Egor Kuznetsov, Makar Artemov, Ogunwoye Faith Ouwatobi, Marcelino Fernando, Artem Asanov, et al. · submitted: March 16, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D4 (tactile fusion strategy), D7 (flow matching coordination).

D4 is the primary target. D4.v1 is late fusion at the hand head, with the tactile sensor present at inference. HapticVLA demonstrates that tactile-aware action generation can be learned offline and deployed WITHOUT any tactile sensor at inference, via two tightly coupled stages: Safety-Aware Reward-Weighted Flow Matching (SA-RWFM) trains a flow-matching action expert incorporating precomputed contact-safety tactile rewards; Tactile Distillation (TD) then distills a compact `tactile_token` from that teacher, training a student VLA to predict it from vision and proprioception only. The student achieves 86.7% mean real-robot success on contact-rich tasks — outperforming baseline VLAs that do have tactile sensors at inference. This directly challenges the premise underlying D4.v1's inference-time sensor dependency.

D7: SA-RWFM is a flow-matching teacher consistent with D7.v1's independent per-head flow model. The TD stage is a distillation wrapper only and does not alter the denoising schedule.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

Training a VLA to be tactile-aware via reward-weighted flow matching and then distilling away the sensor dependency entirely at deployment — unlike TacFiLM and Shared-Autonomy Arm-Hand VLA (both tracked, both require tactile sensors at inference), HapticVLA produces a deployed policy that achieves 86.7% real-world success on contact-rich gripper tasks without any on-board tactile hardware, and outperforms sensor-equipped inference-time baselines.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

If CP2 real-world trials reveal that dexterous-hand tactile sensors are unreliable or unavailable for in-hand rotation, HapticVLA's 2-stage distillation is a viable D4 fallback. In `hand_head_cfg.py`, add `tactile_training_mode = "distillation"`: Stage 1 trains the hand expert with a sim-side tactile-reward signal (SA-RWFM); Stage 2 distills a compact vector (target size: 128 dim, consistent with HapticVLA's architecture) from the teacher into the student without sensor input at inference. The concrete metric to add to the CP2 evaluation protocol: `hand_contact_accuracy_no_sensor / hand_contact_accuracy_with_sensor` — HapticVLA achieves ratio > 1.0 on gripper contact tasks; targeting > 0.85 for dexterous in-hand rotation would be the CP2 pass threshold.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

HapticVLA evaluates a parallel-jaw gripper with a single, relatively low-dimensional contact signal. A dexterous 20-DoF hand requires multi-finger distributed contact geometry — the compact `tactile_token` (128-dim vector) may be too low-dimensional to capture fingertip-level contact differentiation needed for in-hand rotation and tool manipulation. Cheapest sanity check at CP1: apply tactile distillation only to the wrist/palm force signal (1D force, analogous to HapticVLA's gripper contact), measuring whether the student hand expert retains > 80% of the teacher's wrist-force accuracy in sim. If this single-signal distillation fails, the compact-token bottleneck must be widened before attempting full-finger distributed contact distillation.

## 🥉 Paper 3 — PRIORITY ★

**HiVLA: A Visual-Grounded-Centric Hierarchical Embodied Manipulation System**
[arXiv:2604.14125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14125) · Tianshuo Yang, Guanyu Chen, Yutian Chen, Zhixuan Liang, Yitian Liu, Zanxin Chen, Chunpu Xu, Haotian Liang, et al. · submitted: April 15, 2026 · source: keyword-sweep

### 🎯 (a) P# / D# touched

P1 — D7 (flow matching coordination), D8 (sharing module mechanism).

D7: HiVLA implements a strict two-level hierarchy — a VLM planner produces (subtask instruction + target bounding box) at low frequency, then a flow-matching Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action expert executes at high frequency. This is the most concrete published prior-art implementation of D7.v1 (body completes planning → output → lower expert denoises conditioned on that output) applied to a planning-vs-execution split rather than an anatomical arm-vs-hand split.

D8: HiVLA's DiT action expert is conditioned via cascaded cross-attention over 3 ordered streams: (1) global context tokens, (2) high-resolution object-centric crop tokens, (3) skill-semantic tokens from the VLM planner. This is a concrete (β) multi-layer + multi-stream variant of D8 option (C) cross-attention, empirically validated on real-robot fine-grained manipulation of small objects in cluttered scenes.

### ✨ (b) What is genuinely new

Cascaded cross-attention within a DiT action expert that serially integrates conditioning in fixed order (global → object-centric → skill-semantic) — unlike π0/π0.5's flat cross-attention over all VLM tokens at once, the ordered decomposition isolates what each attention pass attends to, outperforms flat cross-attention baselines on out-of-distribution fine-grained tasks in both simulation and real-world experiments, and preserves the VLM's zero-shot reasoning by decoupling planning entirely from low-level execution.

### ⚙️ (c) Decision implication

HiVLA's 3-stream cascaded cross-attention provides a concrete template for the D8 sharing module if the hand head is redesigned as a DiT before CP1. In `hand_head_cfg.py`, if switching to transformer-style hand head, set `hand_conditioning_streams = ["a_b", "hand_crop_vision"]` as a 2-stream cascaded cross-attention: body action `a_b` conditions first (coarser body intent), then a 64×64 hand-region visual crop conditions second (finer local state). The ordering follows HiVLA's coarse-to-fine principle and avoids the flat-pool cross-attention limitation. Metric to watch: per-finger contact precision on in-hand rotation vs. single-stream FiLM(`a_b`) baseline.

### ⚠️ (d) Failure mode to probe first

HiVLA's object-centric crop benefits manipulation of small objects seen in the wrist/global camera. The P1 hand expert needs contact-level information — tactile signals or finger proprioception — not a camera crop of the object. The cascade benefit may not appear until high-resolution hand-mounted cameras or tactile sensor tokens are available (CP2+). Cheapest check before CP1: run 2-stream cross-attention (`a_b` + hand visual crop) vs. single-stream FiLM(`a_b`) in sim in-hand rotation and measure whether the crop token adds any signal over noise in the hand trajectory.
