## 1. Scope [STABLE structure, LIVING content] [AGENT-INPUT]

**Owns**: the questions of what a policy carries **across steps** and how a
task longer than one skill is **structured in time**. **Memory** — which past
observations the policy keeps, for how long, and in what container: a short
window, a compressed episode summary, or weights updated at test time.
**Decomposition** — where a long task is split into stages, and what crosses
each boundary: a language subtask, a goal marked in the image, a predicted
contact subgoal, a skill expert. **Progress** — how the policy knows which
stage is under way. **Recovery** — what returns it to the task after a slip.
Language lands here as a carrier of subtasks. The neighbours keep their
own: how a single step's observation is built is P3's, a goal a world model
supplies is P4's (D3FQ), skill experts as a split of the decoder are P5's
(D3GS), the clock inside one chunk is P6's, adaptation across tasks is P2's
(D3RP), and recovery a human makes by taking over is P0's (D5VN, D8LC).

**Thesis tie** — why this axis is asked: a policy that reads only the
current frame cannot tell what it has done from what is left, and a longer
history can become a shortcut it copies instead of reading the scene —
whether a dexterous, multi-stage task needs memory, an explicit stage
structure, or neither, and what each costs, is open. The current frame alone
and one undivided policy stay threads, the baselines a memory or a
decomposition is measured against.

**Questions**: what the policy carries across steps (D5GY), where a long
task is split (D3HN), how the policy knows where it is (D7KC), what returns
it to the task (D4WB).

**Premises** — the policy is a pretrained VLA or an imitation-learned
visuomotor policy, and the hand is the one `context/P3.md` §1 Premises sets
out.

**Scouting lens** — three tiers, and a paper is placed on this ladder before
it is scored:

1. **Memory or stage structure measured on a multi-stage task** — a policy
   result reported with and without the history, the split or the progress
   signal, on a task whose stages must be sequenced. The north star.
2. **Any memory, subtask or progress mechanism in a manipulation policy** —
   gripper, arm and bimanual included, because what a memory or a split buys
   transfers to the hand. The evidence pool.
3. **Long-horizon benchmarks and planners that structure a task without
   changing the policy** — surfaced as the comparison group a learned memory
   or split is measured against.

Recall runs wide across all three; the ranking anchor stays tier 1.

**Anti-topics** (noise filter — excluded unless an unusually strong tie to
P7 or a P7 question):

- Language or VLM task planners evaluated on symbolic actions, with no
  low-level manipulation policy in the loop
- Navigation, embodied question answering and mobile long-horizon tasks with
  no manipulation
- Memory or retrieval for language agents outside robot control
- Long-horizon suites whose length comes only from chaining pick-and-place,
  with no memory, decomposition or progress measurement
- RL from scratch, or policies trained in simulation and transferred to
  hardware, with no pretrained VLA or imitation policy in the loop
- Survey / position papers (read manually, not via agent)

## 2. Open Questions — P7 [LIVING] [AGENT-INPUT]

The questions this pillar keeps open. Each names what it turns on and the
threads the corpus has followed so far; none is waiting to be closed, and a
thread is added when a paper opens one.

#### [D5GY] 정책은 지나간 일을 얼마나, 어떤 형태로 기억하나 — 지금 장면만, 최근 몇 초, 에피소드 전체의 요약 (P7)
- One frame cannot tell what is done from what is left, a long one can become a shortcut, and each memory fixes its size in advance. Threads: the current frame alone; a short window of past states (2608.03052); the last second of motion queried by the present (MotionVLA); the past track drawn on the image (TraceVLA); episode force folded into tokens (FM-VLA); history written into weights at test time (RoboTTT).
#### [D3HN] 긴 과제는 어디서 단계로 나누나 — 언어로 된 하위 지시, 이미지 위 다음 목표 표시, 나누지 않음 (P7)
- One instruction unrolls into stages, and where the split falls decides what each level is trained on and what has to cross the boundary. Threads: language subtasks handed down by a planner (TouchWorld); the next stage's goal marked on the image by the VLM, labelled where the gripper state changes (AVP); one undivided policy.
#### [D7KC] 정책은 과제가 어디까지 진행됐는지 어떻게 아나 — 진행도 값, 지난 단계의 기록, 장면만 보고 (P7)
- Which stage is under way decides when to switch, retry or stop, and a policy that cannot tell repeats or skips a stage. Threads: a normalized grasp-progress value placed inside the action (InDex); a high-level memory of past subtasks and predicted outcomes (TouchWorld); a short hand-command history read as the motion phase (LAMP); progress left to the observation.
#### [D4WB] 실행이 어긋나면 정책은 어떻게 과제로 되돌아오나 (P7)
- On a long task an early slip compounds, and what the policy does next decides whether one error costs a stage or the episode. Threads: self-correction within the episode against the previous chunk's unexecuted tail (SALT); a failure detector that triggers a retry; restarting the episode.
