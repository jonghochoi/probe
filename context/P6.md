## 1. Scope [STABLE structure, LIVING content] [AGENT-INPUT]

**Owns**: the questions of the **action's time axis** — how fast and how
smoothly a trajectory unrolls on its way to the actuator. **Clock** — at
what rate each modality arrives and actions leave. **Execution speed** —
where it is set: in the data, as an input, by a choice of temporal
resolution, or after the policy by retiming its path. **Continuity** — how
consecutive chunks join without a jump the hardware feels as jerk. A paper
lands here when the time profile of the motion is what it varies or
measures; how the decoder is split, a fast loop beside a slow backbone
included, is P5's, and which metrics a benchmark reports is P1's.

**Thesis tie** — why this axis is asked: a policy trained by imitation
inherits the tempo of its demonstrations and the clock of its slowest
modality, and whether human-level speed and smooth motion are learned,
conditioned or recovered after the policy — and what each costs where contact
is made — is open. Demonstration speed and chunks replaced whole stay threads
of D3MR and D0BZ, the baselines a faster or smoother execution is measured
against.

**Questions**: on what clock signals arrive and actions leave (D1MZ), where
execution speed is set (D3MR), how chunks join (D0BZ).

**Premises** — the hand is the one `context/P3.md` §1 Premises sets out.

**Scouting lens** — three tiers, and a paper is placed on this ladder before
it is scored:

1. **Timing measured on dexterous execution** — a paper that changes a clock,
   where execution speed is set or how chunks join, and reports wall-clock
   time, a speed ratio to the demonstration or a smoothness quantity beside
   success on a contact-rich or multi-finger task. The north star; a paper
   that *measures* what a policy-rate loop can and cannot hold lands here too.
2. **Timing on any other embodiment** — the same changes on arm-only,
   gripper or bimanual tasks. The evidence pool — what a speed-up or a
   boundary fix costs transfers to the hand.
3. **Trajectory timing outside a learned policy** — time-optimal path
   parameterization, retiming and smoothing of a planner's or a fixed
   policy's path, surfaced as the **comparison group** a learned speed is
   measured against.

Recall runs wide across all three; the ranking anchor stays tier 1.

**Anti-topics** (noise filter — excluded unless an unusually strong tie to
P6 or a P6 question):

- Inference acceleration (token pruning, quantization, caching, distillation)
  that reports model latency but never the executed motion
- Faster or smoother motion claimed without a measured wall-clock time, speed
  ratio or smoothness quantity
- Locomotion or whole-body gait timing with no manipulation in the loop
- Survey / position papers (read manually, not via agent)

## 2. Open Questions — P6 [LIVING] [AGENT-INPUT]

The questions this pillar keeps open. Each names what it turns on and the
threads the corpus has followed so far; none is waiting to be closed, and a
thread is added when a paper opens one.

#### [D1MZ] 감각 신호는 어떤 주기로 들어오고, 행동은 어떤 주기로 나가나 (P6)
- Modalities arrive at different rates and contact changes faster than vision, so which signal sets the clock stays open. Threads: one shared rate; proprioception every tick, vision-language asynchronous (πR²); tactile refreshed inside the chunk (TacForcing); a tactile expert re-denoising mid-chunk (T-Rex); the first action finished in one step (FASTER); a reflex loop below the policy.
#### [D3MR] 정책의 실행 속도는 어디서 정해지나 — 시연 데이터, 속도 입력, 정책 출력 뒤의 시간 조정 (P6)
- Imitation inherits the demonstrations' tempo, and running faster can cost precision where contact is made. Threads: demonstration speed kept; demonstrations sped up where actions are loose (DemoSpeedup); speed as a conditioning input (TempoVLA); temporal resolution routed per scene (PaceVLA); the path reshaped in time after the policy (Realtime-VLA V2); time-optimal retiming under joint limits (TOPP-RA).
#### [D0BZ] 행동 청크 사이를 어떻게 매끄럽게 잇나 — 통째로 교체, 겹쳐서 평균, 앞 청크에 이어 생성 (P6)
- Plans switch at a chunk boundary, and a jump there reaches the hardware as jerk. Threads: chunks replaced whole; overlapping chunks averaged (ACT); the next chunk inpainted against the committed prefix (RTC); the prefix pinned ahead of a per-position noise ramp (πR²); the next chunk integrated from where the last one ended (ODE Policy); jerk spread across the chunk after the policy (Realtime-VLA V2).
